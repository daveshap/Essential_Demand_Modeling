Useful distinctions in the source, offered as interpretation aids rather than new mandatory criteria:

- **Lines 183–223**: an occupation bundles multiple reasons for demand; a human premium is not automatically a separable percent of revenue. "Essential" does not mean essential to survival.
- **Lines 259–269 and 934–968**: accountable human authorization differs from compensation paid by a company/insurer. A narrow approval role does not make every supporting task essential. Pure legal designation and substantive review are distinct.
- **Lines 970–1008**: care contains lifting, medication, hygiene, administration and companionship. Outcome advantages and a preference for actual human company can coexist.
- **Lines 1010–1044**: teaching contains explanation, practice, feedback, supervision, relationships and certification. Human students and peer relationships are not the same thing as demand for paid human teachers. A current staffing rule and a voluntary human premium are different reasons.
- **Lines 1046–1086**: human sports, ritual and named human performance can be constitutive; ticketing, lighting, scheduling and support operations remain separate. Paid athlete/artist demand is not inherited by every associated job.
- **Lines 1088–1132**: haircuts, tours, restaurants, advisers and training are mixed bundles. Reliability, location or technical competence alone do not establish nonfungibility. Artisan provenance can apply to some customers/products without applying to a whole industrial occupation.
- **Lines 1584–1624**: distinguish preference rate, premium, purchased volume, paid labor requirement and income. Current jobs multiplied by judgment coefficients are job-weighted scenario equivalents, not observed essential jobs, FTEs or a forecast of jobs surviving automation. Do not count unpaid family/peer participation as paid worker demand.
